# Regimen Reachability (2026-11-18)

The unattended regimen's rule vocabulary promised reach its scan pipeline never delivered.  This change makes every rule honest: a schedulable rule now selects at least one category the regimen's standard-tier scan includes, and every category sits in exactly one state -- schedulable, ask-first-only, or no rule at all.

## What changed

- **AI & Agent Junk (aiArtifacts) is standard-tier.**  Its scan gates every item by age, rotation, or archival, and pre-selects only abandoned BotFleet rollback packages past a day-long grace period.  The regimen deletes only pre-selected items, so the agent-state rule -- which a stored regimen could already carry, to no effect -- finally reaches exactly those verdicts.  Its transcripts and logs stay unselected and manual.
- **Orphaned App Leftovers, Local AI & LLM Models, and Large & Old Files are ask-first-only.**  They answered to xcode-artifacts, a schedulable name the Extreme tier blocked on every run.  Their verdicts are heuristics over user data (an uninstalled app's folders, model weights with no staleness gate, large files in personal folders), not proofs of regenerability, so they keep the manual Extreme surface and the ask-first label, like tool-made backups and stale temp repos.
- **Dead rule names removed.**  app-staging, sqlite-wal, brew, and simulator-runtimes belong to the scripts/hoghunter-clean engine; no DiskCleaner category answers to them.  They are out of schedulableRules and defaultRules.  sanitized() intersects stored rules with schedulableRules, so stored regimens self-migrate.
- **Defaults: snapshots and tmp-files are default rules, and no category is excluded by default.**  Local snapshots pinning deleted blocks were the force multiplier in the Nov 16 and Nov 17 disk emergencies; the old apfsSnapshots default exclusion silently vetoed thinning even for an owner who enabled the rule.  Stored exclusions are user state, are not rewritten, and still win.  Precision on tmp-files: the regimen's scan is rule-gated by category (CleanRegimenRunner derives the scanned categories from the enabled rules), so the tmp-files default pulls the tempFiles category into every default regimen's scan -- a /tmp walk on each run.  Deletions are a second gate: scanTempFiles pre-selects nothing ("idle plain files have no inferred backup status"), so the default selects no deletions today and no temp file is deleted unattended by this change; it takes deletion effect the moment a future scan verdict pre-selects provably disposable temp items.
- **The snapshots rule lifts its rows past the pre-check filter.**  Snapshot rows scan unselected (the manual panel's don't-pre-check default), and the runner cleans only pre-selected items -- a third gate under the two the audit found.  Thinning is one volume-wide tmutil call, so a row's pre-check state never was a per-item verdict.  With the rule enabled, snapshot rows ride beside the rule-selected items, the way the Trash valve's rows do -- except the lift never removes a stored category exclusion from the scan, so an owner's exclusion keeps veto power.  One hardening followed from review (Seer, PR #375): snapshot rows clean in a separate final batch after every file item, because thinning purges the run's own safety snapshot and a mixed chunked batch would have deleted its files after the rollback point was gone.

## Effect on the owner's stored state

Defaults are fresh-state only.  His stored regimen keeps its rules (dead names drop at sanitize); its agent-state rule starts reaching AI artifacts' pre-selected items once this is installed.  His stored apfsSnapshots exclusion stands, and his stored rules do not include snapshots, so snapshot thinning on his machine still needs his own two settings changes.  His Trash valve (emptyTrashBelowGB) is unset, so the regimen never empties his Trash.

## The 41 GiB Trash was not Hog Hunter

Investigation, same night: Trash went from empty (Nov 16) to 41 GiB in about a day -- some 150 timestamped copies each of the same cache directories (copilot x112 at ~190 MB, _cacache x143, io.tailscale.ipn.macsys, com.apple.python, ShipIt, MiniMax Agent, CloudKit, org.swift.swiftpm).  Hog Hunter is not the refiller.  Its unattended engines delete outright (the reclaim engine uses find -depth -delete and unlink, never rm -rf; the Vacuum uses rmtree/unlink), and the Swift regimen is interval-bounded (24 h) and pre-selection-bounded, so it cannot produce ~150 copies a day.  DiskCleaner.clean does move items to the Trash -- that is the manual clean's design (safety snapshot first, Trash put-back) -- and the regimen shares that path within its bounds.

The refiller is the housekeeper chain: com.jay.mac-resource-watch (LaunchAgent, 300 s tick) drives janitor.sh, whose low-free branch -- firing every tick while free space sat under its threshold (26-28 GiB free against a 30 GiB crit line on Nov 17) -- runs the CleanMyMac CLI's dev/ai/trash sweeps with --force next to the Hog Hunter engine, plus its own rm -rf calls.  The swept bytes land in ~/.Trash instead of being freed (CleanMyMac's move-to-Trash clean semantics, and the mavis-trash interception this repo's reclaim-policy.json already documents for rm-style deletes), so free space never recovered, the branch kept firing, and the Trash compounded.  That chain is fleet tooling outside this repo; this change does not alter it.  Separately: the Hog Hunter test suite's clean() tests trash fixture directories into the real ~/.Trash (small; observed ~47 MB of fixtures after a full-suite run) -- a test-hygiene note, not this event.
